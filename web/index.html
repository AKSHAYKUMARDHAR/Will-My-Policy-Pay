3. If your claim is cut

About 41% of the health complaints the Insurance Ombudsman decided in 2024-25 ended in the policyholder's favour or in a settlement. Challenging a deduction is worth it when a rule was broken.

  1. Ask for the reasons in writing. When a claim is cut, the insurer must explain each deduction with the policy clause it relies on (IRDAI Master Circular on Health Insurance, 29 May 2024, para 17b).
  2. Write to the insurer's Grievance Redressal Officer. Their email is on your policy and the insurer's website. Attach the bill, the deduction sheet and the clause you rely on. Keep the acknowledgement.
  3. Escalate on Bima Bharosa (bimabharosa.irdai.gov.in), IRDAI's grievance portal, if the reply doesn't resolve it.
  4. Go to the Insurance Ombudsman (cioins.co.in) if the insurer rejects your complaint or doesn't reply within a month. It's free, for claims up to ₹50 lakh, and you have a year from the insurer's reply.

Rules that often decide a dispute

  • A proportionate deduction for a costlier room can't touch medicines and consumables, implants, diagnostics or ICU charges, and can't be applied by hospitals that don't price by room category (IRDAI/HLT/REG/CIR/151/06/2020).
  • After 60 months of continuous cover, a claim can't be rejected for non-disclosure, except for proven fraud (Master Circular 2024, para 13).
  • Final authorisation for discharge is due within 3 hours of the hospital's request; charges from a longer delay are the insurer's (para 16).
  • Items IRDAI lists as non-medical (gloves, attendant food, toiletries) are not payable unless you have a consumables cover, so those deductions are usually correct.
